## How to ask ChAi questions about your customers

Ask ChAi is built for digging into specifics — questions that go beyond what a single risk breakdown or the Today screen shows you at a glance.

To use it:

1. Open **Ask ChAi** from within the app.
2. Type your question in plain language — no special syntax needed.
3. ChAi will respond based on the customer and business data connected to your account.

You can ask about a single customer, a group of customers, or a trend across your customer base — Ask ChAi isn't limited to one-at-a-time lookups.

---

## What Ask ChAi can and can't tell you

Ask ChAi can help you understand and explore the data connected to your account — things like risk patterns, changes over time, and context behind a customer's score.

**What it doesn't do:**

- Ask ChAi doesn't script what to say to a customer. It can help you understand *why* a customer is at risk, but deciding how to reach out and what to say is down to your judgment.
- Ask ChAi's answers are only as good as the data connected to your account — if a data source isn't connected, or a customer has limited data history, its answers about that customer will reflect that gap.
- Ask ChAi answers based on your business's data — it isn't a general business advisor or a source of information outside what's in your account.

> *Note: Awareness of data sufficiency within Ask ChAi's answers (i.e. flagging when it doesn't have enough signal to answer confidently) is an area of ongoing development.*

---

## Getting the most out of Ask ChAi with good questions

Ask ChAi tends to give more useful answers the more specific your question is. A few ways to get better results:

- **Be specific about scope.** "Which customers are at higher risk than last month?" gets a more useful answer than "how are things going?"
- **Ask follow-up questions.** If an answer raises a new question, ask it — Ask ChAi is built for back-and-forth, not just single lookups.
- **Anchor questions in what you've already seen.** If you've just looked at a customer's risk breakdown, asking a follow-up question about that same customer will usually get you more context than starting from scratch.
- **Ask about patterns, not just individuals.** Questions like "what's common among my highest-risk customers this month?" can surface things a single risk breakdown won't.
